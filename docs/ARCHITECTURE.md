# Architecture and implementation boundaries

## Chess core

`Position` maintains a mailbox, per-colour and per-piece bitboards, side to move,
castling rights, FEN en-passant square, clocks, material/positional accumulators
and an owned vector of per-ply states. Moves update them incrementally; unmake
restores the captured piece from the state being popped and restores the
previous key, counters and accumulators exactly. There are no pointers into the
state vector. Castling rights are updated for king/rook moves and rook captures
on home squares. `make_null_move()`/`unmake_null_move()` are exact inverses:
they flip the side, discard the en-passant right and its key contribution, and
leave the fullmove counter alone (unit test `position_extra/null_move_*`).

Leaper attacks are precomputed; sliding attacks use multiplication-indexed
bitboards. The multipliers are generated by our deterministic
`tools/gen_magics.cpp`, not imported from another engine. Initialisation checks
every occupancy subset for destructive collisions, and tests compare the tables
with a separate ray walker. Legal generation handles pins and check evasions
without allowing king captures; king moves use the occupancy with the king
removed. En passant uses the full post-capture occupancy for king safety.
Castling tests the empty path and the king transit squares.

FEN parsing is transactional and checks six fields, board shape, kings, pawns on
back ranks, side, counters, rights and en-passant consistency. It rejects
adjacent kings and a side-not-to-move king left in check. This is
structural/rule validation, **not proof of a reachable game history**. FEN
counters above 10^12 are rejected. FEN preserves a raw double-push target even
when no capture exists.

A fixed-seed Zobrist hash covers pieces, turn, castling rights and an en-passant
file **only if a legal capture is available**. A stored FEN starts a new known
history; `position ... moves ...` records subsequent history. Position
equivalence for repetition uses that hash. The core distinguishes
current/intended-move threefold and fifty-move claims from automatic fivefold and
75-move draws, checkmate and stalemate.

### Dead positions (Article 5.2.2)

`src/chess/dead_position.{hpp,cpp}` implements a general decision procedure:
cheap impossible-material proofs, a cooperating "helpmate" witness search, and
a bounded exhaustive enumeration of the complete reachable state component
(128-bit state identity over placement, side, castling rights and en-passant
square). It returns `Alive` only with a checkmate witness, `Dead` only after a
complete enumeration proved that no checkmate is reachable, and otherwise
`Unknown`. The full specification, correctness argument, measurements and
limits are in [DEAD_POSITIONS.md](DEAD_POSITIONS.md).

The engine uses the detector only for adjudication (`draw_status()` with a small
proof budget, and the CLI `deadpos` command). The search keeps using the cheap,
sound and deliberately incomplete `insufficient_material()` predicate, so the
cost of the exact analysis never affects playing strength, and the engine never
declares a FIDE draw on its own.

## Search and evaluation

One worker performs iterative deepening over negamax alpha-beta with principal
variation search. dev2 adds, each behind a `SearchParams` switch that the UCI
options `NullMove`, `LMR`, `Aspiration`, `Futility`, `ReverseFutility`,
`SEEPruning` and `CheckExtension` expose (all default on, so a GUI that never
sends them sees the released behaviour):

* **aspiration windows** around the previous iteration's score, widening on a
  fail-high/fail-low;
* **null-move pruning** with a material guard, a "not directly after a null
  move" guard and mate-window suppression;
* **late move reductions** using an integer reduction schedule, with a full-depth
  re-search when a reduced move beats alpha;
* **reverse futility pruning** at shallow depth, suppressed inside mate windows;
* **futility pruning** of late quiet moves;
* **static exchange evaluation** (SEE) for capture ordering and pruning, and
  **delta pruning** in quiescence;
* **mate-distance pruning** and a three-extension cap on check extensions;
* killer, history and hash-move ordering with a selection-sort move picker, so
  a cutoff never requires re-sorting the list;
* a soft/hard time budget with an early stop when the best move has been stable.

Quiescence searches all evasions when in check and captures plus all promotion
choices otherwise; stalemate is detected before a static score is returned.
There is no tablebase, opening book or parallel search. Mate scores are
distance-normalised when stored in the transposition table. The table is bounded
by the `Hash` setting and protected from incompatible reversible histories,
fifty-move clocks, search-root history boundaries and check-extension budgets;
this conservative context reduces cross-path hits. Zobrist collisions remain
theoretically possible.

Search scores are from the side-to-move perspective. Search assumes claimable
draws are taken; a repeated position inside the search tree is treated as a draw
to break cycles. That is a search heuristic, not a declaration that a twofold
position is a FIDE draw. Terminal mate is checked before a move-count draw. An
interrupted search retains the last completed iteration's best move or a legal
root fallback. Depth/node/time bounds can be combined; node limits are hard caps.
Time management reserves a communication allowance and uses soft and hard
budgets; it does not predict opponent speed or network latency.

Evaluation is independently written, handcrafted and non-neural. It combines
material and formula-derived piece-square values with tapered
middlegame/endgame weights, pawn isolation/doubling/passed-pawn features,
bishop pair, mobility, rook files, pawn shelter and king-zone attacks. The
numbers are heuristic, not trained or tuned weights. The evaluation is clamped
below mate scores.

## Protocol and concurrency

The main thread reads UCI and serialises output. A single search worker owns a
position copy. `stop` is an atomic flag; position changes, option changes, new
games and quit join the worker before changing shared state. `isready` does not
stop it. Tables are initialised once; TT access is confined to the joined
worker. No stdout diagnostics are emitted outside UCI `info string`/protocol
responses. Unknown `go` tokens are ignored as the UCI specification requires;
malformed values of supported parameters still reject the command. A
ThreadSanitizer CI job exercises this hand-off.

The engine advertises `Hash` plus the boolean ablation switches above and
deliberately does **not** advertise `Threads`: the search is single-threaded, and
claiming a `Threads` option that does nothing would be worse than not offering
one. Single-thread deterministic behaviour is a release requirement, and dev2
adds no parallel search.

## Tooling

* `stockbear perft`, `bench --depth|--nodes|--movetime [--param feature=0|1]`
  print CSV with a deterministic `# signature` fingerprint, so behaviour changes
  are detected without comparing timings; `--param` runs the ablations.
* `stockbear deadpos --fen ... [--states N] [--depth N] [--no-exhaustive]`
  adjudicates Article 5.2.2 and prints the verdict, state counts, timings, the
  witness and whether the witness ends in checkmate.
* `scripts/tournament.py` plays paired-opening matches with colour reversal,
  python-chess adjudication, PGN/JSONL/CSV output, per-engine option overrides
  (`--a-option`/`--b-option`) and honest uncertainty (`scripts/match_stats.py`:
  Wilson, pentanomial over opening pairs, normal-approximation SPRT).
* `scripts/dead_position_reference.py` and `scripts/dead_position_corpus.py`
  provide the independent cross-check and the regression corpus.

## Deliberate limits

- Standard chess only; no Chess960, pondering, `searchmoves`, MultiPV, opening
  book or tablebases.
- No NNUE/transformer/policy network, training, MCTS or GPU inference.
- Very long paths can reach the explicit `MAX_PLY` search horizon; the engine
  returns a heuristic score there.
- Exact dead-position analysis is exponential in the worst case and reports
  `Unknown` when its budget is exhausted rather than guessing.
- Repetition prior to the supplied FEN is unknowable; supply the move sequence
  where possible.
- Clock management and evaluation are hand-tuned heuristics. Strength claims are
  limited to the recorded matches and their stated uncertainty.
